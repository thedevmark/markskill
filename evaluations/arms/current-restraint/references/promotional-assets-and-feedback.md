# Promotional Assets and Iterative Feedback

Use this reference for store listing images, promo tiles, thumbnails, screenshot carousels, ads, social graphics, or repeated visual revision. These surfaces fail differently from an application screen: they are scanned at reduced size and distance, so product-detail density becomes noise.

## Start from the viewing condition

Define the real viewing condition before composing:

- search thumbnail at its displayed size, not only the source resolution;
- marquee or banner from normal viewing distance, including roughly two feet from a desktop display;
- carousel image while moving between adjacent slides;
- social image inside the feed crop where it will appear.

Judge the exported pixels in that condition. A high-resolution source that is legible only while zoomed in has failed.

Run both tests:

1. **Distance test:** step back or reduce the preview until it approximates the distribution context. The identity, dominant promise, and proof result must remain readable.
2. **Squint test:** blur detail mentally. The image should retain one focal message and one proof shape, not dissolve into equal-weight text and borders.

## Give each asset one job

- **Thumbnail:** identity first. Use the canonical mark or lockup at useful size plus one compact descriptor. Do not turn it into a miniature feature slide.
- **Marquee/banner:** billboard, not interface documentation. Use one strong promise and one immediately readable proof. Four or five textual elements across the entire asset is already a meaningful density budget; exceed it only when the distribution context proves it remains effortless.
- **Carousel slide:** one claim beside one proof artifact. A slide earns its place by communicating a distinct, strong capability, not merely by being different from the prior slide.

Do not fill a carousel quota with setup trivia, account-state details, defensive controls, or generic reassurance while stronger differentiating behavior is available.

## Preserve typography roles

- Reuse the canonical brand asset and wordmark contract: actual font file, weight, tracking, line height, smoothing, and asset proportions. A word typed near an approximate icon is not the logo.
- Load bundled fonts explicitly before rendering. A fallback face is a defect, not a harmless implementation detail.
- When the user assigns a distinctive display, mono, pixel, or serif face to a role such as descriptor or subtext, keep it in that role. Consistency does not mean spreading it to headlines, labels, buttons, and product UI.
- Treat headline, subtext, label, status, and product copy as semantic roles. A correction to one role must not leak into the others.

## Make product proof causal

The demonstration must visibly substantiate the adjacent claim.

- **Autofill:** show completed fields and an obvious completion result. Do not require reading a fake applicant profile.
- **Automatic learning:** show the sequence: user enters an unknown safe answer, the product saves it automatically, and the next application reuses it. Do not present “left blank” as the feature when the product's stronger behavior is learning from the user's action.
- **Tailoring:** show the transformation path or change receipt: posting requirements mapped to existing evidence, strongest matches moved, relevant material surfaced, or a before/after difference. A generic résumé page with one colored line does not prove tailoring.
- **Multi-step automation:** show progress moving across pages or states, not a static form plus explanatory copy.
- **Coverage:** show the real supported contexts or platforms. Avoid universal language when the implementation has a bounded set.

Do not invent a person to make the proof feel real. Avoid fake names, email addresses, phone numbers, locations, portfolio URLs, employers, testimonials, metrics, and outcomes unless the artifact is explicitly labeled and the invented content is necessary and harmless. Prefer anonymized value bars, structural fields, source-grounded example data, or mechanism-level receipts. Never let placeholder content become an implied customer claim.

## Flatten the visual hierarchy

Audit the visible border tree. Name every enclosing layer from the background inward. If the proof reads as background → browser → application → card → popup → pill, the hierarchy is carrying implementation history instead of meaning.

- Put proof directly on the color field when an outer frame adds no information.
- Use at most one enclosing product frame for a billboard-scale asset unless a second boundary is essential to understanding the interaction.
- Prefer large rows, crops, rules, whitespace, and direct labels to panels inside panels.
- Remove browser chrome, addresses, status pills, footnotes, helper copy, secondary buttons, and metadata when they are not the claim.
- Crop screenshots to focus on the action and state that prove the claim. Label simplified reconstructions as illustrations; do not pass edited controls or invented states off as an actual product capture.

Count text elements and borders after the first render. If the user asks for “much less text” or calls out boxes within boxes, do not merely reduce font size, shorten sentences, or restyle the same nesting. Delete whole layers and secondary messages.

## Handle repeated feedback without regression

Before each revision, update an internal ledger with:

- canonical assets and exact brand contracts;
- accepted hierarchy and typography roles;
- required copy or capability story;
- rejected concepts and phrases;
- size, crop, spacing, and alignment decisions still in force;
- truth constraints such as no invented personal data or metrics.

Then compare the proposed change against every item. Do not reintroduce a rejected idea under new wording. Do not “fix” one asset by breaking the established system across the others.

When the user repeats a correction, treat repetition as evidence that the workflow failed, not as another isolated preference. Identify the missing general rule, apply it across the full asset set, and add a regression check when the project supports one.

Do not make the user absorb a stream of “you are right” messages while the same category of error recurs. In an active revision, respond with the diagnosis, the concrete correction, and verified output. If the premise is still unclear, pause the rendering loop and re-ground the concept before producing more variants.

## Promotional-asset completion gate

- At real display scale or distance, can a viewer read the identity, promise, and proof result without leaning in?
- Is the thumbnail identity-first, the marquee billboard-simple, and each carousel slide limited to one claim plus one proof?
- Is every font confined to its approved semantic role?
- Does the product proof demonstrate the mechanism rather than decorate the claim?
- Are customer claims grounded, with any necessary fictional examples clearly labeled and harmless?
- Does any safety or limitation copy appear only where consequential, rather than occupying the feature headline?
- Is there no unnecessary frame inside a frame, card inside a card, or popup inside a product shell?
- Have all accepted decisions from prior feedback survived this revision?
- Were final exports regenerated, inspected as pixels, and checked at the distribution context—not only in source?
